## Crashes are part of the deal

Here is something most people are never told plainly: **if you invest for decades, you will live through several crashes.** Not maybe. Definitely.

US stocks fall 10% or more about once every year or two on average. They fall 20% or more every several years. A few times in a lifetime, they fall 50%.

Every one of those US crashes has eventually recovered. Some took months. Some took years. The investors who did worst were usually not the ones who held through. They were the ones who sold near the bottom.

## Why we sell at the bottom

Crashes feel different from the inside. Headlines say it is different this time. Your balance is down more than a year's salary. Friends are selling. Every part of your brain says "get out before it gets worse."

That feeling is normal and human. It is also usually wrong. By the time it feels safe to buy again, prices have often already recovered.

## What to do in a crash

Write this down somewhere you will see it:

1. **Do nothing new.** Keep your regular payday investing going. Your money now buys more shares for the same price.
2. **Check your cash.** Your emergency fund is the reason you won't need to sell. If you are retired, spend from your cash bucket.
3. **Rebalance if a slice is far off.** After a big drop, stocks may be well below target. Moving some bonds or gold into stocks is the plan working. It feels scary. It is the "buy low" part.
4. **Look less.** Checking your balance daily changes nothing except your stress. Once a month is plenty.

## What not to do

- **Don't sell everything** to "wait it out." You have to be right twice: when to get out and when to get back in. Almost no one is.
- **Don't stop investing.** Pausing contributions during a crash means skipping the cheapest prices you may see for years.
- **Don't swap plans mid-crash.** If you truly can't handle the swings, change your mix to "steadier" after things calm down, not at the bottom.

## If you are near or in retirement

A crash hurts more when you need to sell to live. That is why the plan holds more bonds and cash as you get older. Your cash bucket is designed to cover about two years of spending, so you can leave stocks alone while they recover. The retirement chapter covers this in detail.

## A thought that helps

Prices falling is the price you pay for higher long-term returns. If stocks never crashed, they wouldn't pay more than a savings account. The drops are not a sign the plan is failing. They are the reason it works.
