## Reading changes nothing. Doing does.

You understand the plan now. This chapter turns it into actions. Most people can do all of this in one afternoon. After that, the plan needs about an hour a year.

Every brokerage looks a little different. The steps are the same everywhere.

## Walkthrough 1: open a high-yield savings account

1. Compare a few online banks. Look for **FDIC insurance**, no monthly fees, and a competitive interest rate.
2. Open the account online. You'll need your ID and Social Security number.
3. Link it to your checking account.
4. Set up an **automatic transfer** on payday until your emergency fund is full.

## Walkthrough 2: set up your 401(k)

1. Log in to your employer's benefits site, or ask HR who runs the 401(k).
2. Find **contribution rate**. Set it to at least the percent needed to get the full match. Choose Roth or traditional if both are offered.
3. Find **investment elections** or **future contributions.** Pick the funds that match your mix (see "Buying" for what to look for), and set the percentages.
4. Look for a way to **change existing investments**, sometimes called "rebalance" or "exchange." Use it to move money you already have into your chosen funds.
5. Check your **beneficiary**.
6. Optional: turn on automatic yearly increases, like 1% more each year.

**Common mistake:** setting new contributions but forgetting existing money. Many people have old money sitting in a default fund for years.

## Walkthrough 3: open a Roth IRA

1. Pick a large, low-cost brokerage. Fidelity, Schwab, and Vanguard are popular choices. All three are fine.
2. Choose **open an account**, then **Roth IRA** (or traditional IRA).
3. Fill in your details and link your bank account.
4. Transfer money in. It will arrive as cash.
5. **Buy your funds.** Search the ticker (for example, VTI), choose **buy**, enter a dollar amount, and confirm. Repeat for each fund in your mix.
6. Set up **automatic deposits** from your bank, and **automatic investing** into your funds if your brokerage offers it.
7. Turn on **dividend reinvestment.**
8. Add a **beneficiary**.

**The big mistake to avoid:** money transferred into an IRA does **not** invest itself. It sits as cash until you buy something. People have found years of cash sitting uninvested in their IRAs.

## Walkthrough 4: open an HSA (if eligible)

1. Check if your health plan is a high-deductible plan that qualifies. Your benefits site will say if an HSA is offered.
2. Set your payroll contribution. Payroll contributions also skip some payroll taxes, which makes them even better.
3. Most HSAs let you **invest** once you have a minimum balance in cash. Find the investment option and choose your funds.
4. Save receipts for medical costs you pay out of pocket. You can pay yourself back later, tax-free.

## Walkthrough 5: set your yearly reminder

1. Pick a date. Your birthday or New Year's Day works.
2. Put a yearly reminder on your calendar called **Money check-up**.
3. On that day:
   - Add up all your accounts and enter them in the rebalance tool.
   - Rebalance if anything is off by more than its limit.
   - Check if you can raise your savings rate.
   - Check whether your life stage changed.
   - Check new contribution limits for the year.
   - Look over your beneficiaries.

That is the whole yearly job.
