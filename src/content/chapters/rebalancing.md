## Your mix drifts

You set a mix. Then the market moves. Stocks have a great year and grow from 65% of your money to 72%. Bitcoin doubles and becomes 10%. Without doing anything, you are now taking more risk than you chose.

[[Rebalancing]] puts things back where they belong.

## Why it works

To get back to target, you **trim what went up** and **add to what went down.** That is "buy low, sell high," done by a rule instead of a guess.

It also keeps your risk where you set it. Your mix after a long bull market won't quietly turn into an all-stock portfolio right before a crash.

## When to do it

Pick one simple rule and stick with it:

1. **Once a year, on a set date.** Your birthday or New Year's Day works well. Put it on your calendar.
2. **Or when a slice drifts too far.** More than 5 percentage points off its target. For small slices like Bitcoin, more than half of its target size. (A 5% target is "too far" below 2.5% or above 7.5%.)

Most of the time, the yearly check shows only small drift. Then you do nothing. That is a normal year.

Don't rebalance every time something moves. Small drifts don't matter, and frequent trading can cost taxes and fees.

## How to do it without paying taxes

**Retirement accounts (401(k), IRA, HSA):** sell and buy freely. There is no tax on trades inside them.

**Taxable brokerage accounts:** selling a winner can trigger [[capital gains]] tax. So first, try to fix drift **with new money**. Send your next few contributions to whatever is underweight. Only sell if new money won't close the gap.

**All accounts together:** if you hold the same things in several accounts, do the selling inside the retirement accounts, where it is tax-free.

## A few tips

- **Rebalance your total,** not each account separately. Your 401(k) can be all stocks and bonds as long as your IRA covers the rest.
- **Round to make it easy.** Getting within a percent or two is plenty.
- **Write down what you did.** A line in a note: date, what you sold, what you bought. Next year you'll thank yourself.
