## You can't plan what you can't see

Most people do not know where their money goes. Pay comes in, bills go out, and at the end of the month there is less left than expected. That is not a character flaw. It is just what happens without a plan.

You do not need a strict budget. You need three numbers.

## Number 1: what comes in

Use your **take-home pay**. That is what lands in your bank account after taxes, insurance, and retirement deductions. Not the salary on your offer letter.

If you are paid every two weeks, multiply one paycheck by 26 and divide by 12. That gives you a monthly number. (A few months will have three paychecks. Treat those as a bonus.)

If your income changes month to month, use the average of the last 6 to 12 months. Then plan with a slightly lower number to be safe.

## Number 2: what must go out

These are your **fixed costs**. You pay them every month, no matter what:

- Rent or mortgage
- Utilities, phone, internet
- Car payment, insurance
- Minimum debt payments
- Child care

These are hard to change quickly. But they are the biggest lever over time. A cheaper apartment or car can do more than skipping a hundred coffees.

## Number 3: what you choose to spend

These are your **flexible costs**: food, gas, shopping, eating out, subscriptions, fun.

Don't guess this one. Look at last month's bank and card statements and add it up. Most people are surprised. That is the point. You can't fix what you don't see.

## Your savings rate

Now put it together. Whatever is left is what you can save.

Your **savings rate** is the share of take-home pay you keep. It is the single most important number in this whole plan. More than which funds you pick.

- **Under 5%:** a start. Look for one big cost to cut or one way to earn more.
- **10% to 15%:** solid. This is a common target for a normal retirement.
- **20% or more:** strong. This can mean retiring earlier or with more room.

Note: money already going into your 401(k) through payroll counts as savings too. Add it in when you think about your rate.

## Tips that actually work

- **Pay yourself first.** Set savings to move automatically on payday, before you can spend it.
- **Check once a month.** Ten minutes with your statements beats a complicated app you stop using.
- **Raise savings when pay goes up.** When you get a raise, save half of it. You won't miss money you never got used to.
- **Use separate accounts.** One for bills, one for spending, one for savings. It makes the numbers obvious.
