## The snowball

Roll a small snowball down a hill. As it rolls, it picks up more snow. The bigger it gets, the more snow it picks up with each turn. By the bottom, it is huge.

Money works the same way. That is [[compounding]].

## How it works

Say you invest $1,000 and it grows 7% in a year. Now you have $1,070.

The next year, you earn 7% on $1,070, not just on the original $1,000. That is $75 instead of $70. A small difference at first.

But keep going. Your gains start earning their own gains. After 10 years, $1,000 becomes about $2,000. After 30 years, about $7,600. You never added another dollar.

## Time is the secret ingredient

The amount you invest matters. **Time matters more.** The longest-running money does most of the work.

Play with the tool below. Try moving only the years and watch the gap between what you put in and what you end up with.

## A quick note on returns

The tool uses a yearly growth rate you choose. Here is a sense of what is realistic:

- **US stocks** have grown about 10% a year on average over the last century. After inflation, that is about 7%.
- **A mixed portfolio** with bonds, gold, and cash will grow slower but more smoothly.
- **The rest of this site** uses cautious numbers after inflation, about 3% to 5% for a whole mix. That way, every dollar figure is in today's money, and we do not promise more than is fair.

No one knows what the next 30 years will bring. The point is not the exact number. The point is that steady money plus time is powerful.

## What this means for you

- **Start now, even small.** At 7% a year, waiting 10 years cuts what the same monthly amount grows into by about half.
- **Don't interrupt it.** Pulling money out to "wait for a better time" resets the snowball.
- **Fees and taxes slow it down.** Small yearly costs compound too, in the wrong direction. You will see how to keep them low in later chapters.

If you are older and feel like you missed the boat, you did not. Compounding works over any stretch of 10 or more years. A 50-year-old may still have 30 or more years of investing ahead, because the money keeps working through retirement.
