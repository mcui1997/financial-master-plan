## The one question that matters

Picking a mix sounds hard. It comes down to one question: **when will you need this money?**

If it is decades away, you can ride out crashes. You lean toward growth. If you need it soon, a crash could force you to sell at a loss. You lean toward protection.

So your mix follows your **life stage**.

## The three stages

| | Building | Close to retirement | Retired |
|---|---|---|---|
| When | More than 10 years to go | 10 years or less | Living off savings |
| Grow (stocks + Bitcoin) | 70% | 53% | 42% |
| Hedge (gold + REITs) | 15% | 15% | 15% |
| Protect (bonds + cash) | 15% | 32% | 43% |

The hedge stays about the same. Money moves from Grow to Protect as you get closer to needing it.

Inside each job, here is the full mix while building:

- US stocks 46%, international stocks 19%
- Bitcoin 5%
- Gold 10%, REITs 5%
- Bonds 15%

## Two small adjustments

**Comfort.** Some people sleep fine through a 30% drop. Others don't. You can shift 10% between stocks and bonds:

- **Steadier:** 10% less in stocks, 10% more in bonds.
- **Bolder:** 10% more in stocks, 10% less in bonds (bonds never go below 5%).

Be honest here. The right mix is one you will actually hold through a crash. A bold mix you abandon in a panic is worse than a steady one you keep.

**Style.** Prefer the classic approach? Switch to **classic** and the plan uses only stocks, bonds, and cash. Don't want Bitcoin? Turn it off and that slice moves to stocks.

## Money you need soon stays out

The mix is for long-term money. **Anything you need within about 5 years stays in cash or T-bills,** no matter your age. That includes a house down payment, a car, a wedding, or tuition next year.

Why 5 years? Stocks have sometimes taken that long, or longer, to recover from a crash.

## When to turn the dial

You don't need to adjust every year. Change your mix when you cross a stage line:

- About **10 years before** you plan to retire, move to "Close."
- **When you retire,** move to "Retired."

Make the change gradually over a year or two if you like. Use new money and your yearly tidy-up to get there. In retirement accounts, you can also just sell and buy with no tax cost.

## What about target-date funds?

A [[target-date fund]] does all of this for you in one fund. You pick the year you plan to retire. It holds stocks and bonds and gets safer over time on its own.

They are a great choice, especially inside a 401(k). The main differences from this plan: they hold no gold, REITs (beyond what the stock funds own), or Bitcoin, and some charge higher fees. If you want the simplest possible path, a low-cost target-date fund is a fine answer.
