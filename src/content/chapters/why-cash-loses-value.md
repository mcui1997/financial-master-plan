## The grocery cart that keeps getting pricier

Think about a cart of groceries you bought ten years ago. The same cart costs a lot more today. The food did not get better. Your dollars got weaker.

That is [[inflation]]. Prices across the economy rise a little almost every year. In the US, it has averaged around 3% a year over the long run. Some years are calm. In 2022 it went above 9%.

3% sounds small. It adds up. At 3% a year, prices roughly double every 24 years.

## What that does to your savings

Inflation does not take dollars out of your account. The number stays the same. What changes is how much each dollar buys.

If your savings earn less than inflation, you are slowly losing ground, even though the balance never goes down. That is why money under the mattress feels safe but isn't.

## Why does this happen?

A few reasons, kept simple:

- **More money chasing the same stuff.** When the government and banks create a lot of new money, prices tend to rise.
- **Rising costs.** When wages, energy, or materials cost more, companies raise prices.
- **It is the goal, a little.** The US central bank, the Federal Reserve, aims for about 2% inflation a year. They see a little inflation as healthy for the economy.

The takeaway is not that the system is broken. It is that **holding only cash is a slow loss by design.**

## But isn't cash safe?

Cash is safe from crashes. It is not safe from inflation. Different risks.

A stock fund might drop 30% in a bad year and recover over the next few. Cash never drops in a bad year, but loses a little every single year, forever.

Over a few months, cash is the safer choice. Over a few decades, investing has been the safer choice.

## So why keep any cash at all?

Because cash has real jobs that nothing else does well:

- **Emergencies.** A car repair, a layoff, a hospital bill. You need money now, in full, no matter what the market is doing.
- **Money you need soon.** A down payment next year should not be in stocks.
- **Calm.** Knowing you have a cushion makes it much easier to leave your investments alone during a crash.

So the answer is not "no cash." It is **the right amount of cash, and the rest invested.** You will figure out your right amount in the chapter on safety.

## Where your cash should live

When you do hold cash, put it in a [[high-yield savings account|hysa]] instead of a regular one. Many pay several times more interest. They are just as safe when the bank has [[FDIC insurance|fdic]], which covers up to $250,000 per person, per bank.

This will not beat inflation every year. That is fine. Its job is to be there, not to grow.
