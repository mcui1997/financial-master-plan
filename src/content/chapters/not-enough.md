## Who this plan fits

This plan is built for most US workers with a regular paycheck, saving through a 401(k), IRA, and maybe an HSA and brokerage account. That covers a lot of people.

Some situations need more than a general guide. If one of these is you, the plan is still a good foundation. You will just need extra help on the parts below.

## Situations that need more

**You are self-employed or have side income.** You have different accounts available, like a SEP IRA or Solo 401(k), which can let you save much more. You may also need to pay taxes quarterly.

**You earn a high income.** Above the Roth IRA income limits, you can't put money into a Roth IRA directly. There is a well-known workaround called the "backdoor Roth," but it has tax traps. Higher earners also face more complex tax planning.

**You get paid in company stock.** RSUs, stock options, or an ESPP come with their own tax rules and timing decisions. A big chunk of your wealth in one company, the same one that pays your salary, is also a big risk.

**You have a pension.** A pension changes how much you need to save and how much risk you can take. It may also come with lump-sum vs. monthly payment choices that are hard to undo.

**You inherited money or accounts.** Inherited IRAs come with special withdrawal rules and deadlines.

**You are going through a big life event.** Divorce, a death in the family, a serious illness, or selling a business. Decisions made in these moments can be hard to reverse.

**You own a business or rental properties.** Taxes, retirement accounts, and risk all work differently.

**You live outside the US.** Every account in this plan is US-specific. The ideas still apply, but the accounts and taxes don't.

## Finding good help

If you hire a professional, look for these words:

- **[[Fiduciary]]:** legally required to act in your best interest. Ask directly: "Are you a fiduciary at all times, in writing?"
- **Fee-only:** paid only by you, not by commissions on products they sell you.

Common ways to pay:

- **Hourly or flat-fee planners:** you pay for a plan or a few hours of advice. Good value for most people who want a second opinion.
- **Assets under management:** a yearly percent of your money, often around 1%. On large balances, that adds up to a lot. Make sure the service is worth it.

**Warning signs:** pushing whole life insurance or annuities quickly, promising returns, high-fee funds, or not being able to explain fees simply.

For tax questions, a **CPA** or **enrolled agent (EA)** is the right person.

## Checking a professional

You can look up advisors on the SEC's adviser search and FINRA's BrokerCheck. Both are free and show their record.
