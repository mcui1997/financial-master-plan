## Stop trying to time it

Everyone wants to buy at the bottom and sell at the top. Almost no one can do it, including professionals. Markets often jump the most right after the scariest days. Miss a handful of those days and your returns drop a lot.

So this plan never asks you to guess.

## Buy a little, again and again

Invest a set amount on a set schedule. Every payday is easiest. This is called [[dollar cost averaging|dca]].

- When prices are high, your money buys fewer shares.
- When prices are low, the same money buys more shares.
- Over time, you end up buying more when things are cheap, without trying.

The real power is not the math. It is that **the decision is already made.** No checking the news. No waiting for the right moment.

## Got a big lump sum?

A bonus, an inheritance, or a rollover? Research shows that investing it all at once usually comes out ahead, because markets rise more often than they fall.

But if putting it all in at once would keep you up at night, spread it over 3 to 6 months. Slightly lower expected return, much better sleep. Either is fine. Just pick one and set it on a schedule.

## Set it and forget it

Automation is what makes this plan work for regular people.

- **401(k):** set your contribution percent and pick your funds once. It happens every paycheck.
- **IRA and brokerage:** set up an automatic transfer from your bank on payday, plus automatic investing into your funds. Most brokerages offer this.
- **Dividends:** turn on dividend reinvestment, so payouts buy more shares.

## Fees matter more than you think

Every fund charges a yearly fee called the [[expense ratio]]. It is taken out quietly, so you never see a bill.

- The funds above mostly charge 0.03% to 0.25% a year. That is $3 to $25 per $10,000.
- Some funds and advisors charge 1% or more. That is $100 or more per $10,000, every year.

The difference compounds. Over 30 years, a 1% yearly fee can take about a quarter of your final balance. Same investments, less money for you.

**Rule of thumb:** look for funds under about 0.25%. For plain stock and bond index funds, under 0.10% is easy to find.

## Inside a 401(k)

Your 401(k) has a fixed menu. You may not see these exact funds. Look for the closest match:

- "S&P 500 Index" or "Total Stock Market Index" for US stocks
- "International Index" or "Total International" for international stocks
- "Bond Index" or "Aggregate Bond" for bonds
- "Stable Value" or "Money Market" for cash

Most 401(k)s have no gold, REITs, or Bitcoin. That is fine. **Treat all your accounts as one big portfolio.** Hold stocks and bonds in the 401(k), and put gold, REITs, and Bitcoin in your IRA to make up the total mix.

If the menu is short on good index funds, a low-cost target-date fund is a solid fallback.
