## Everything flips

For your whole working life, the plan is simple: buy a little every payday. In retirement, the paychecks stop. Now you **sell** a little every year to live.

That one change brings new risks. The good news is a few simple rules handle them.

## How much do you need?

Start with what you will spend each year in retirement. Then subtract any steady income, like Social Security or a pension. What is left has to come from your savings.

A common starting rule is the [[4% rule|four-percent-rule]]. In your first year of retirement, take out 4% of your savings. Each year after, take the same dollar amount, adjusted for inflation. Historically, that has lasted at least 30 years in most US scenarios.

Flip it around and you get your **[[FI number|fi-number]]**: yearly need × 25.

- Need $40,000 a year from savings? Your FI number is about $1,000,000.
- Need $60,000? About $1,500,000.

The 4% rule is a starting point, not a promise. Retiring early (40 or more years of spending) calls for a lower rate, like 3% to 3.5%. Being flexible helps a lot: spending a bit less in bad years makes your money last much longer.

## The biggest danger: a crash early on

Imagine two people retire with the same savings. Over 30 years, they get the exact same yearly returns, just in a different order.

- One gets a crash in year one.
- The other gets the crash in year twenty.

The first person can end up far worse off. They had to sell stocks at low prices to pay the bills, and those shares are gone for good. This is called [[sequence of returns risk|sequence-risk]]. It is the main risk of retirement.

## The fix: three buckets

Split your money by **when you will spend it**:

1. **Cash bucket: about 2 years of spending.** High-yield savings, money market, or T-bills. This is what you live on.
2. **Bond bucket: about 5 to 8 more years.** Steady money to refill the cash bucket.
3. **Growth bucket: the rest.** Stocks, gold, REITs, and a little Bitcoin, for the long run.

Once a year, refill the cash bucket:

- **Good year for stocks?** Sell some stocks to refill cash.
- **Bad year for stocks?** Refill from bonds or gold instead. Leave stocks alone to recover.
- **Very bad stretch?** Just spend the cash bucket down. That is why it holds two years.

This is your yearly rebalance and your paycheck in one step. The retired mix in this plan is built to match these buckets.

## Social Security

Most US workers get Social Security. It is a monthly check for life, adjusted for inflation. That makes it very valuable.

- You can start as early as **62**. Your check is permanently smaller.
- At your **full retirement age** (67 for anyone born in 1960 or later), you get your full amount.
- Every year you wait past full retirement age, up to **70**, your check grows by about 8%.

Waiting often makes sense if you are healthy and can live on savings for a few years. Get your personal estimate at **ssa.gov** by creating a free "my Social Security" account.

## Other things to know

- **Required withdrawals.** Starting in your 70s (age 73 or 75, depending on your birth year), you must take a minimum amount out of traditional 401(k)s and IRAs each year. These are called [[RMDs|rmd]]. Roth IRAs don't have them for the original owner.
- **The Roth conversion window.** The years between retiring and starting Social Security can be low-income years. Some people move money from traditional to Roth accounts then, paying tax at a low rate. This can be worth a lot. It is a good topic for a tax professional.
- **Health insurance before 65.** If you retire before Medicare, plan for health insurance costs. They can be large.
- **Inflation keeps going.** Plan to spend from savings for 30 years or more. That is why the retired mix still holds stocks and gold, not just bonds and cash.

## Helping a parent who is retired

Many people reading this are managing money for a parent. The same rules apply. Two things to watch: they usually need **more** protection, not less, because they have fewer years to recover from a crash. And they need a plan they can follow if you are not around. Keep it simple and write it down.
