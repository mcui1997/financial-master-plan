## Think in jobs, not tickers

There are thousands of things you can invest in. You only need a few. The trick is to stop asking "what will go up?" and start asking **"what job does this do for me?"**

This plan uses three jobs:

- **Grow:** build wealth over decades.
- **Hedge:** hold value when inflation or money printing hurts other assets.
- **Protect:** stay steady and pay the bills when times are bad.

Each job has one or two building blocks.

## Grow: stocks

A [[stock]] is a small piece of a real company. When the company earns more, your piece is worth more. Many companies also pay part of their profits to owners. That is a [[dividend]].

Stocks are the main engine of this plan. Over long periods, they have grown more than anything else. US stocks have returned about 10% a year on average over the last century, about 7% after inflation.

The catch: they are bumpy. Stocks drop in about one of every four years. Every several years, they drop 20% or more. Once in a while, 50%.

You won't pick single companies. You'll buy [[index funds|index-fund]], which own hundreds or thousands of companies at once for a tiny fee. If one company fails, you barely notice.

The plan splits stocks two ways:

- **US stocks, about 70% of your stocks.** One fund that owns nearly the whole US market.
- **International stocks, about 30% of your stocks.** Companies in Europe, Japan, and emerging markets. The US is around 60% of the world's stock market. Some decades the US leads, some decades it doesn't. Owning both means you don't have to guess.

## Grow: a small slice of Bitcoin

Bitcoin is a digital asset with a fixed supply. Only 21 million will ever exist. No government or company can make more. Some people see it as "digital gold."

**Why it is here:** it has grown enormously since 2009, and a growing number of large investors now hold it. Since 2024, you can buy it through a regular [[ETF|etf]] in any brokerage account.

**Why it is small:** it is extremely volatile. It has fallen more than 75% several times. It could fail completely. Nobody really knows what it should be worth.

So the plan holds **5% at most**, less as you get older. If it soars, it helps a lot. If it goes to zero, the other 95% carries you. You can also turn it off in the mix builder. The plan works fine without it.

**Honest note:** most mainstream advisors would not include Bitcoin at all. This plan does, in a small dose, by choice.

## Hedge: gold

Gold has held value for thousands of years. It pays nothing. It just sits there. That is the point.

Gold tends to hold up when people lose trust in money: high inflation, heavy money printing, financial panic. In 2022, when stocks and bonds both fell, gold was roughly flat. In 2008, it rose while stocks fell almost 40%.

But gold can also go nowhere for a decade or more. It is insurance, not an engine. The plan holds **10%**.

**Honest note:** many advisors hold little or no gold. This plan holds some as a hedge against the very inflation that the first chapter warned about.

## Hedge: real estate (REITs)

A [[REIT|reit]] is a company that owns buildings: apartments, warehouses, offices, cell towers. By law, it must pay out most of its income to owners. Rents tend to rise with inflation over time.

You won't buy property. You'll buy one fund that owns REITs around the world. The plan holds a small **5%** tilt.

**Honest note:** a total stock market fund already owns some REITs. This is a small extra tilt toward real assets, not a must-have.

## Protect: bonds

A [[bond]] is a loan. You lend money to the government or a company. They pay you interest and return your money later.

Bonds move much less than stocks. In most stock crashes they hold steady or even rise. Their job is to cushion the ride, give you something to sell when stocks are down, and fund retirement.

**About 2022:** that year, bonds fell about 13%. It surprised many people. When [[interest rates|interest-rate]] jump fast, existing bonds lose value. Two things to know. First, a total bond fund holds bonds of many lengths, so it recovers as old bonds are replaced by new ones paying higher rates. Second, after 2022, bonds pay much more interest than they did in 2021. Bonds are not risk-free. They are low-risk.

The plan's bond share depends on your life stage, from 15% while building to about a third in retirement.

## Protect: cash and T-bills

Cash in a high-yield savings account, a [[money market fund]], or short-term government loans called [[T-bills|t-bill]]. It barely moves in price at all.

Inside the plan, cash only matters near and in retirement. It is the money you'll spend in the next couple of years, so you never have to sell other things at a bad time. (Your emergency fund is separate and not part of the mix.)

## Why mix them at all?

Because they don't all fall at once. In 2008, stocks crashed while bonds and gold rose. In 2022, stocks and bonds fell while gold held. No single asset wins every year. A mix means something is always holding up.

This is called [[diversification]]. It is the closest thing to a free lunch in investing.

## How this compares to the classic approach

The most common simple plan is the **three-fund portfolio**: US stocks, international stocks, and bonds. That's it. It is a great plan, and millions of people use it.

This plan adds gold, REITs, and a little Bitcoin. The idea is to be better protected against inflation and money printing, which the classic plan does not handle as well. The tradeoff is more moving parts and two assets (gold and Bitcoin) that don't produce income.

Both are reasonable. In the mix builder, you can switch to the classic version and compare.
